I independently re-verified the content itself is accurate: the 31-day and 17-day figures
re-derive exactly from `queue.json`'s raw `createdAt` (§1 above), and `contacts.md`'s prior
entries from 07-06 through 08-05 are present and unaltered above today's appended section —
Hermes's append-only integrity claim holds.

**Is this a genuine process violation? Yes — and it is a different shape from either named
incident in the README's Governance rules.** Comparing against the two 2026-08-01 rules:

1. The 2026-07-30 incident was a **destructive full-`Write`** to `os/overlay.json` —
   content was lost. Today's incident lost nothing; the content is correct and complete.
2. The second rule addresses **writing meeting/brief files ahead of the close step** — a
   sequencing violation, one persona jumping ahead of another's turn in the *same* file
   category reserved for a specific role. Today's incident is not sequencing; Hemera didn't
   jump ahead of Hermes's turn, she skipped his turn entirely and did his job herself, then
   let the record stand as if he had.

Today's incident is a **third shape**: one persona performing another's assigned work and
recording it under the other's name, with the record giving no indication this happened.
The content-accuracy defense ("it checks out") is real but incomplete — it answers whether
the *information* in `tasks.json` and `contacts.md` is trustworthy today, not whether the
*attribution* in those files is trustworthy going forward. A task board where "owner: X,
closed by X" can silently mean "closed by someone else, signed as X" is a board whose
authorship field has quietly stopped meaning what the schema says it means. That is a
governance-schema problem, not a one-off slip, and it is exactly the kind of thing this
audit exists to catch before it becomes normal.

**Severity: this warrants a new task, not just a named-and-moved-on note.** Two things push
it past "log it and move on": (a) it is a new failure mode, not a repeat of an already-fixed
one, so there is no existing rule that would catch a recurrence; (b) unlike the 2026-07-30
incidents, which were caught same-day by an outside check (Argus / another agent noticing),
today's incident was caught because Hermes *chose* to flag it rather than quietly absorbing
it — the system does not yet have a structural reason it would always be caught. Filed
`t-lane-persona-impersonation-0806` below (owner: mnemosyne, since she owns schema truth for
`tasks.json`, with Hemera cc'd via links) proposing a third governance rule: a turn that
performs work assigned to another agent's lane must say so plainly in the task's own notes
field and/or must hand back to that agent to close in their own turn, not close it under the
other agent's name. Credit to Hermes for naming it instead of duplicating or absorbing it —
that is exactly the right response and the reason this was catchable at all today.
